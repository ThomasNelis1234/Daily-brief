## Intro
Good morning Thomas, it's Thursday the fifth of November. Today: the Belgian state and Engie miss their nuclear deadline and set a new one for December. In America, Democrats win the Senate as well as the House. And Anthropic quietly extends its hidden watermark to older Claude models, with a European rule in the background. Let's get into it.

## AI
We start with AI, and with a feature you can't see.

A quick note first. This podcast is written with Claude, which is made by Anthropic, so I'll stick closely to what the sources say.

On Tuesday, Anthropic updated the release notes of its developer platform. Text generated by three older models, Claude Mythos 5, Claude Opus 4.7 and Claude Sonnet 4.6, now carries Anthropic's text watermark. Emergent, a tech news site, reported the change on Wednesday and counts a fourth model, Opus 4.6. The watermark first arrived on Anthropic's newest models in August.

How does a watermark work on words? There are no hidden characters and no metadata. According to Anthropic's own explanation, reported in August by the Free Press Journal, when Claude has several equally natural words to choose from, it picks among them using a secret mathematical key instead of ordinary randomness. Over a long enough text, that leaves a statistical pattern. Only someone holding the matching detector key can check for it. Anthropic says the mark doesn't change the meaning, quality or readability of a response.

There are real limits, and Anthropic has flagged several itself. A detected mark shows Claude may have processed the text, not that Claude wrote it, because people also use it to translate, summarise or proofread. Very short or narrowly factual passages may carry no mark at all. Heavy paraphrasing, translation or rewriting by another model can weaken it. And according to the release notes, on Sonnet 4.6, developers who change certain sampling settings, which control how random the word choice is, may get text without a watermark.

Why now? The European Union's AI Act. Its transparency rules, in what's known as Article 50, apply since the second of August this year. One of them requires providers of generative AI to mark their output in a machine-readable way. According to legal summaries of the final Digital Omnibus text, systems already on the market before August get a grace period for that marking, until the second of December. The timing of Anthropic's move lines up with that date. Emergent reports that Anthropic applies the watermark worldwide, with no opt-out, and that detection is limited to authorised parties such as regulators and researchers.

Staying with build or buy, Meta has bought a coding startup. TechCrunch reports that Meta acquired Conductor, a two-year-old company whose tool lets software engineers run several AI coding agents side by side, each in its own workspace, on one part of a project. Interestingly, Conductor didn't build its own models. It ran agents from outside labs, including Anthropic and OpenAI. Meta didn't disclose the price. Founder Charlie Holtz says the team will join Meta's Superintelligence Labs and promises "something new" early next year. For current users, the cloud service shuts down on the twentieth of November, and cloud data will then be deleted. So Meta, which launched its own coding agent in August, is buying the interface layer rather than building it.

Next, a story about the cost of all this. Bloomberg reports, via TechCrunch, that Apple is cutting how much its engineers can spend on AI each day. In October, the daily cap per engineer fell from three hundred to two hundred dollars, according to unnamed sources. Some allowances may drop to one hundred dollars, and staff outside engineering could get as little as twenty-five. Apple hadn't commented. Earlier this year, many companies pushed staff to use as much AI as possible. Now, as the bills arrive, several are rationing tokens instead.

And one more from Apple. According to the newsletter Sam on AI, Apple has opened its new Siri integration to all developers in its latest beta software. Apps can expose their content and actions to Siri, and combine them into what Apple calls skills. Customers should get it in a few months. But note: the new Siri isn't available on iPhones and iPads in the European Union.

So why does the watermark story matter for a product manager? Because compliance is becoming a feature of the model itself, not just of your app. If you build on a large language model and sell in Europe, check which of your models carry a mark, which settings switch it off, and whether your own product owes users a visible label as well.

## Politics
Over to politics, and to Belgium first, where the King's consultations reach their last round.

On Wednesday, King Philippe received the two party leaders often seen as the hardest to reconcile. First came Conner Rousseau of Vooruit. According to La Dernière Heure, he called it a long conversation about how serious the situation is. On leaving the palace, he said: "I told the King that we want an agreement." He added that the deal must be fair, and that everyone must do their share. Vooruit wants the wealthiest to contribute around one and a half billion euros, according to VRT.

Then, at noon, came Georges-Louis Bouchez of the MR. He stayed for about an hour and, as he had promised, said nothing, either on the way in or on the way out. His party has repeatedly said there are still too many taxes on the table.

The mood among the others is mixed. Earlier this week, VRT reported that Maxime Prévot of Les Engagés put the chance of new elections at fifty-fifty. And Anders, the Flemish liberals in opposition, repeated that their eight seats are available for what they call an economically right-wing programme. So far, none of the coalition parties has taken up that offer.

Today at noon, it's the turn of Valerie Van Peel of the N-VA, the last of the five coalition parties. Prime Minister Bart De Wever then returns to the palace tomorrow at half past ten. VRT says the King may announce his decision on De Wever's offer to resign on Friday.

To recap what's at stake, VRT has a useful explainer. The coalition wants to find an extra ten billion euros over the coming years, to reduce a public debt of close to seven hundred billion. The split is classic left against right. The MR and the N-VA oppose new taxes. Vooruit and Les Engagés say the planned cuts to healthcare go too far and that the wealthiest should pay more. Because a budget needs six to eight weeks to pass parliament, Belgium will very likely start twenty twenty-seven on provisional twelfths. That means the state can spend one twelfth of last year's budget each month. It keeps things running, but blocks major reforms. If the King accepts the resignation, the government falls. VRT notes almost nobody expects new elections, but they remain possible.

Staying in Brussels, but at European level. On Wednesday, officials from Ukraine, the United States, the European Commission, Britain, France, Germany, NATO and the World Bank met to discuss Ukraine's reconstruction and long-term security guarantees. FinChannel reports that the Commission hosted the first session, focused on post-war reconstruction and economic prosperity. Ukraine's delegation was led by Rustem Umerov, now head of the foreign intelligence service. No guarantee, package or ceasefire was announced. FinChannel describes the outcome so far as procedural rather than substantive. A separate meeting on guarantees, with NATO, France, Germany, Britain and the United States, is due today. Talks with Russian and Ukrainian delegations follow in Abu Dhabi on the tenth and eleventh of November.

And finally, the United States, where the midterm picture is now much clearer. These are still media projections, not certified results. CBS News projects that Democrats have won control of the Senate as well as the House. In the Senate, Democrats have at least fifty-four seats, up from forty-seven. They flipped seats including Texas, Ohio, Kansas, Iowa, North Carolina, South Carolina and Maine. Alaska and Mississippi were still too close to call on Wednesday afternoon. In the House, CBS estimates Democrats will end up with somewhere between two hundred and fifty-one and two hundred and sixty-eight seats. The Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called it a "generational remake of the Senate map". Hakeem Jeffries is expected to become the first Black Speaker of the House.

The fallout started quickly. CBS reports that Speaker Mike Johnson will not seek another term as Republican leader. Vice President JD Vance blamed the war with Iran for the losses. President Trump called that "largely true", pointing to petrol prices, but said he would do it again. He said he accepts the results and will work with Democrats, while promising to use his veto. Democrats, for their part, list lowering costs, reversing Medicaid cuts, opposing tariffs and ending the Iran war among their priorities. The new Congress takes over in January.

## Energy
Over to energy, and to Belgium first, where the nuclear deadline has come and gone.

On Wednesday, the Belgian state, Engie and its Belgian subsidiary Electrabel announced that they have extended the deadline for agreeing the heads of terms, the main conditions of a deal, to the twelfth of December. That's the deal under which the state would take over all of Engie's nuclear activities in Belgium, as set out in a letter of intent from April. In their joint statement, they say months of talks have brought progress and better mutual understanding. But they also say that "significant differences remain", notably about the valuation of the transaction. They describe it as one extra month at most. La Libre notes that the announcement comes as the De Wever government itself risks falling over the budget.

What is the gap about? According to Brussels Signal, citing Belgian reporting last week, dismantling the reactors is estimated at eleven to twelve billion euros, while Engie has set aside about eight point seven billion and has signalled it won't go further. The same report says the Prime Minister has had draft laws prepared to nationalise the plants by force if talks fail, with compensation set by the state and open to challenge in court. That remains a contingency plan, not a decision. Meanwhile, Brussels Signal reports, the advisers Rothschild and KPMG are studying whether the reactors could run longer, at what cost and on what legal basis. And it's still open whether the state would run the plants itself or bring in an industrial partner, with the French group EDF among the options mentioned.

Staying with Belgium, a decision that did go through. On Monday, the federal regulator CREG approved a discount on network costs for Belgium's biggest industrial electricity users. According to La Libre, about fifty companies, including BASF and Nyrstar, will get fifty to seventy per cent off their distribution costs, from the first of November and through twenty twenty-seven. The estimated cost is one hundred and thirty to one hundred and sixty million euros. The aim is to bring their bills closer to those of competitors in Germany and France. To keep tariffs unchanged for households and small businesses, Elia's tariff reserves will be used, and excise duties on electricity should be cut. The industry federation Febeliec called it a step in the right direction.

That fits a wider European debate. On Wednesday, the Clean Air Task Force, an environmental group, published its assessment of the European Union's Electrification Action Plan from July. The plan sets an indicative goal of forty-six per cent of final energy use coming from electricity by twenty forty, and encourages member states to make industrial electricity no more than twice the price of gas by twenty thirty. The group calls the plan a real step forward, but says the price gap between electricity and gas is still the main barrier for industry, and that progress depends heavily on national governments.

And finally, gas prices. According to Yahoo Finance, the Dutch benchmark, known as TTF, rose one point six per cent on Wednesday to ninety-three euros per megawatt-hour. Traders pointed to tensions in the Strait of Hormuz, longer tanker routes for liquefied gas around southern Africa, and a thin storage buffer of around seventy-four per cent. The American election also played a role, as markets weigh whether a Democratic Congress could push Washington towards diplomacy with Iran. That's speculation for now, not a policy change.

## Term of the day
Today's term is the electrification rate. It's the share of all the energy we use at the final stage, in homes, factories and cars, that comes as electricity. Think of your own house. If you heat with a gas boiler and drive a petrol car, your electrification rate is low. Swap in a heat pump and an electric car, and it jumps. It came up today because of the European Union's target of forty-six per cent by twenty forty. There's a twist. Electric machines are more efficient, so when you switch, your total energy use falls too. The Clean Air Task Force points out that this moves both sides of the fraction at once, so the target says less than it seems. Watch the assumptions behind the number, not just the number.
