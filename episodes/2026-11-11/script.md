## Intro
Good morning, it's Wednesday the eleventh of November, Armistice Day, and this is your Daily Brief. ## AI
We start with AI, and with a model that has moved from a locked room into the shop window.

Two weeks ago, Google unveiled Gemini 4 Carbon, which it calls its new frontier model, meaning its most capable one. At first, only a small group of cybersecurity defenders could use it. On Tuesday, Google widened access. According to Google's own blog, developers can now use Carbon through the paid tier of the Gemini application programming interface, the way software talks to the model. Business customers get it too, and subscribers to Google's top consumer plan, AI Ultra, are getting it in the Gemini app and in AI Mode in Search. A separate cyber version remains restricted to vetted security teams.

Google positions Carbon for the hardest work: long debugging sessions, large code migrations and open-ended research. It says the model spends more effort checking its own work. Interestingly, Google itself recommends its cheaper sibling, Gemini 4 Argon, for most everyday software and knowledge work. Google also previewed a feature called swarm in its coding tool Antigravity. There, Carbon acts as the architect and auditor, while faster, cheaper models do pieces of the work in parallel.

Then there's the price. Models are billed per token, a token being a small chunk of text, roughly three quarters of a word. Carbon costs two dollars per million tokens going in and ten dollars per million coming out, as an introductory offer. Google says that doubles at the end of November. VentureBeat points out that even the higher price is half what Anthropic charges for Claude Fable 5.2. A quick disclosure here: this podcast is written with Claude, made by Anthropic, so I'll stick to what the sources say.

And the sources add an important caveat. VentureBeat notes that token price alone is a poor guide. It cites the benchmarking firm Artificial Analysis, which estimates an average cost of under eight dollars per task for Carbon, against more than twenty-six dollars for Fable at its maximum effort setting. On that firm's overall intelligence index, Fable still scores highest, with Carbon just behind. Results also vary by task. Carbon leads on some terminal and automation tests, while Fable leads on larger code changes in real repositories. Several of Google's own claimed wins could not be checked against public tables, VentureBeat adds. On safety, Google says Carbon resists simulated cyberattacks better than Argon, but admits that resistance to prompt injection, where hidden instructions in a document or web page hijack the model, remains an area for improvement.

Staying with the big platforms, OpenAI launched something on Tuesday that looks a lot like a business directory for the chatbot age. It's called Businesses in ChatGPT, and it's in beta. According to The Next Web, companies can claim a verified profile, update their prices and stock, and see how often ChatGPT mentions them and how many people click through to their website. OpenAI says it does not share users' chats with businesses. Eligible American online shops and service companies can also build a Business Agent, a chatbot that answers customer questions inside ChatGPT, clearly labelled and kept apart from ChatGPT's own answers. Those agents must say they are AI, keep prices accurate and avoid pressure tactics. Businesses can also pay to make their agent the landing spot for ChatGPT ads.

Finally, a cautionary tale about the AI supply chain. Notion, the popular workspace app, has told customers that some AI chat transcripts were exposed in a security incident at Fireworks AI, a company that runs models on behalf of other firms. According to CCTest, which reported on Notion's notice, fewer than one per cent of workspaces may be affected. Notion says the transcripts could not be linked to a person or company, and that no other workspace data was involved. Still, it switched off the feature involved, stopped running open-weight models through Fireworks, and advises customers to change any passwords or keys they had pasted into AI chats.

So why does Carbon matter for a product manager? Because the right comparison is no longer price per token but cost per finished task, including retries and human review, and a mix of a strong planner with cheap workers may beat any single model. And Notion's case is a reminder that every model provider you add is also a vendor holding your users' data.

## Politics
Over to politics, and to Belgium first, where the Prime Minister's one-to-one budget talks reach their final round today.

On Tuesday morning, Bart De Wever received Maxime Prévot, vice-prime minister for Les Engagés. According to Business AM, the party's president, Yvan Verougstraete, has stressed that the budget effort of roughly ten billion euros can in no way be achieved through savings alone. Les Engagés, like Vooruit, want a balance between cuts and new revenue. Value added tax is one of the sore points. The MR opposes a general increase, while other coalition partners are open to some tax rises. No details of the meeting's outcome had been published by this morning.

Today it's the turn of Vincent Van Peteghem of CD&V. Business AM reports that he'll bring a member of his cabinet rather than party president Sammy Mahdi. Mahdi says he has full confidence in Van Peteghem, and that party presidents only need to be at the table if a new government agreement is being negotiated. That's a quiet answer to the MR, whose president Georges-Louis Bouchez joined Monday's meeting.

In between his talks, De Wever had a foreign trip on Tuesday. He was due in Maastricht for a Benelux summit, hosted by the Dutch prime minister Rob Jetten, with Luxembourg's prime minister also attending, according to a Dutch government announcement. Meanwhile, the clock keeps ticking, and the opposition keeps up the pressure from the sidelines. De Wever must report to King Philippe again on Monday, the sixteenth of November. Business AM notes that it isn't certain a final agreement has to be on the table by then. And remember, Belgium will already start next year on an emergency budget, in which the government may spend each month no more than a twelfth of the previous year's total.

Staying with budgets, but moving to the European Union. Agence Europe reported that negotiators from the European Parliament and the member states were to make a final attempt on Tuesday to agree the Union's annual budget for twenty twenty-seven. It's the last stage of the yearly process, called conciliation. The two sides are close on the totals, around one hundred and ninety-two to one hundred and ninety-four billion euros in commitments. The main dispute, according to Agence Europe, is who pays the interest on the debt behind the recovery fund and the Ukraine support loan. Agence Europe had earlier reported that France, Germany and Italy want to reduce their contributions. If there's no deal by midnight on Monday the sixteenth, the European Commission must table a new proposal. I couldn't confirm the outcome in the sources available this morning, so we'll come back to it.

Now to the war in Ukraine. On Tuesday, delegations from Ukraine, Russia and the United States sat down in Abu Dhabi for their first three-way talks since February. According to the American broadcaster PBS, the session lasted about four hours. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it constructive and quite substantive, focused mainly on humanitarian issues, with security guarantees also discussed. An unnamed senior Ukrainian official said the sides discussed a large prisoner swap, with details to follow in the coming days. The American envoy Jared Kushner said he saw some good ideas to bridge the differences. Russia's chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said he hoped the meeting would advance the path to peace. A second day of talks takes place today.

The gaps remain large. According to PBS, Russia wants Ukraine to withdraw from four regions Moscow claims to have annexed but hasn't fully captured, and to give up its hopes of joining NATO. Ukraine wants an immediate ceasefire and a direct meeting between Zelenskyy and Vladimir Putin, which Putin has rejected. Russia also objects to Europeans joining the three-way format, though it said European Union representatives could join at a later stage. The Kyiv Independent reports that security advisers from France, Germany, the United Kingdom and Italy, as well as NATO and European Union officials, were in Abu Dhabi for separate meetings.

Speaking at a meeting of the Group of Seven foreign ministers in Paris, the American Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the talks very productive, PBS reports, but urged patience and said a solution is not imminent. It was Rubio's first trip abroad since last week's American midterm elections, in which the Republican Party suffered heavy losses. The Star, citing the Associated Press, notes that those results are likely to constrain the Trump administration's foreign policy. Rubio also dismissed reports that he'll leave the government soon to run for president, saying serving until the end of the term had always been his plan.

## Energy
Over to energy, and to Belgium first, where the North Sea has some good news.

On Tuesday, the European Commission approved Belgian state aid for the first wind farm in the Princess Elisabeth zone, a new area for offshore wind off the Belgian coast. This first lot is known as P E Z one. According to the regional news site Lokaal Nieuws, the federal energy minister, Mathieu Bihet of the MR, called the approval the last obstacle before the tender, the competition between developers, can be relaunched. That should happen soon, but no date was given. Bihet said the file is back on the rails and the preparatory work is nearly done.

Some background. The first tender was halted in twenty twenty-five because of legal, financial and technical uncertainties. The revised version gives builders five years to complete the park instead of four. And the support works through a two-way contract. If the market price falls below an agreed level, the government tops it up. If the price rises above it, the operator pays back the difference. The government says this gives investors more certainty while protecting consumers and public finances. More on that idea in our term of the day.

The power from the zone will come ashore through the Princess Elisabeth Island, the artificial energy island that Elia, the Belgian grid operator, is building at sea. It's a timely step. As we heard on Tuesday, Belgium has been importing record amounts of electricity this year, and its nuclear future still hangs on talks between the government and Engie. According to Engie, both sides have pushed the deadline for their outline agreement to the twelfth of December.

A quick market note. According to Trading Economics, the European gas price slipped to about eighty euros per megawatt-hour on Tuesday, its lowest in a month, after a drop of almost eighteen per cent over the previous week. It remains far above last year's levels, with storage well below normal for the season.

Now to the global stage, and the United Nations climate conference, COP31, in Antalya in Türkiye. Tuesday was Energy Day, and according to Climate Home News, the central idea was electrification, replacing fossil fuels with electricity in cars, homes and factories. The Turkish presidency is pushing a voluntary pledge called thirty-five by thirty-five. It aims to raise electricity's share of the world's final energy use from about twenty-three per cent today to thirty-five per cent by twenty thirty-five. Hürriyet Daily News reports that Fatih Birol, head of the International Energy Agency, backed the push and hopes many countries sign up. The agency will track progress. But Climate Home News notes that the analysts at Climate Action Tracker project only twenty-eight per cent under current policies. So this is a target, not a forecast.

The European Union co-hosted the conference's first Electrification Forum on Tuesday. And Brazil, which led last year's conference, presented a global roadmap to help countries plan their exit from coal, oil and gas. United Nations chief António Guterres urged countries to stop treating that transition as taboo. Some fossil fuel producers are expected to resist putting the roadmap in the final decision. Today, some forty world leaders arrive in Antalya, and organisers have warned of severe traffic disruption.

## Term of the day
Today's term is contract for difference. It's the two-way deal behind Belgium's new offshore wind support. The government and the wind farm agree on a fixed price per megawatt-hour, called the strike price. The farm sells its power on the normal market. When the market price is below the strike price, the government pays the difference. When the market price is above it, the farm pays the difference back. Think of agreeing with a friend to split a taxi at a fixed price: if the meter shows less, you still pay the agreed amount; if it shows more, your friend covers the extra. For developers, it means predictable income and cheaper loans. For consumers, it means the windfall in expensive months flows back to the public purse.
